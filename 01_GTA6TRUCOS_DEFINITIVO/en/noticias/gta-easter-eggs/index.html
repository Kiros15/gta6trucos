The wildest Easter eggs in GTA

From Gouranga to Liberty City’s beating heart

A hidden chocolate egg, a statue with organs and an alien beneath the ice. Explore seven mainline Grand Theft Auto games through their most surprising secrets.

There are two ways to explore a GTA city. The first is to follow the missions. The second begins when you notice a suspicious window, an out-of-the-way path or a door somewhere you seemingly should not be able to reach.

That second approach has rewards of its own. Sometimes you find a joke. Sometimes it is something unsettling that the game never fully explains. And sometimes you discover that the developers anticipated exactly how far your curiosity would take you.

For this tour, we have picked a particularly memorable secret from each mainline entry, with an extra stop in GTA V. The earliest examples are bonuses and playful surprises; later games bring hidden rooms, messages and apparitions. Each gives you a reason to take a detour.

Spoiler warning: we reveal these secrets and where to find them.

01 / Grand Theft Auto

Grand Theft Auto: Gouranga, the original game's mischievous surprise

Long before elaborate mysteries and mountains covered in symbols, the first GTA already had a reward that captured its provocative sense of humour.

Groups of Hare Krishna characters walked through its streets in single file. Running over the whole procession triggered the Gouranga bonus. It was the sort of rule you could discover by accident, turning what seemed like background decoration into part of the scoring system.

There is a useful distinction here: this is not an Easter egg concealed behind a wall. It is a surprise built into the city's behaviour, closer to the logic of an arcade game.

That is what makes it interesting. GTA was still viewed from above, but it was already encouraging you to experiment with the scenery and see what happened. Its world was becoming more than the distance between two missions.

Where to look: watch for lines of Hare Krishna pedestrians walking through the original GTA's streets.

Documentation: Film Stories

02 / GTA 2

GTA 2: Elvis has left the building… and brought some friends

GTA 2 revisited the idea of special pedestrian groups with an even more absurd sight: Elvis Presley impersonators walking through the city in a line.

Quickly eliminating the entire group triggers the Elvis Has Left the Building! bonus. The familiar concert farewell becomes a dark joke inside the game.

The scene works even before you know about the reward. In a city dominated by gangs and police chases, meeting a miniature Elvis procession is already strange enough.

It is a good example of how the early GTA games used pedestrians to give their worlds personality. A few characters, an animation and a message could create a story you would remember long after finishing a mission.

Where to look: look for groups of white-suited Elvis impersonators walking around Anywhere City.

Documentation: GameFAQs

03 / GTA III

GTA III: the hidden sign that catches you looking behind the scenery

The move into three dimensions brought a new temptation: trying to reach every place that seemed to lie outside the intended route.

GTA III responds with one of its best jokes. In a hidden space in Bedford Point, on Staunton Island, a message tells you that you were not supposed to be able to get there.

The contradiction makes it work. You searched for a way in, cleared an obstacle and reached an apparently forgotten corner. Yet someone had already left a note waiting for you.

For a moment, Claude stops being the main character. The message is addressed to the person holding the controller.

There is no grand material reward. The prize is a brief conversation between player and designer: you tried to leave the expected path, and the game acknowledges the effort with a grin.

Where to look: find the Bedford Point car park associated with Kingdom Come, then get over the wall into the concealed space.

Documentation: Grand Theft Wiki

04 / Vice City

Vice City: hunt for an Easter egg and find an actual Easter egg

Vice City takes the expression Easter egg completely literally.

Near the rooftop of the VCN building, a seemingly solid window lets you pass through it. Beyond it is a secret room containing an enormous chocolate egg with an Easter greeting.

The joke works by exploiting a player expectation: when something looks like a closed window, we usually treat it as a boundary.

Passing through it rewards your suspicion with the most literal object possible. You have found an Easter egg inside a video game that hides Easter eggs.

Among so many theories about impossibly elaborate secrets, there is something appealing about this simplicity. You do not need to unravel a conspiracy. You just need to find a room where someone decided to put a giant chocolate egg.

Where to look: reach the VCN rooftop and examine the windows of the neighbouring building. Jump towards the window that lets you pass through the facade.

Documentation: Jeuxvideo.com

05 / San Andreas

San Andreas: climb somewhere impossible, then get told to go away

San Andreas makes the search for secrets the punchline itself.

At the top of one of the towers of Gant Bridge, San Fierro's enormous suspension bridge, a sign announces that there are no Easter eggs up there and tells you to go away.

Naturally, the message is the Easter egg.

The scene feels like a friendly competition. A player sees a huge structure and decides there must be something at its highest point. A designer imagines exactly that reaction and leaves a response where the search will end.

The joke also depends on the effort that came before it. Reading the sign at street level would be less amusing. Discovering it after working out how to get up there makes the teasing memorable.

In a game so well suited to aimless exploration, it is a wonderfully straightforward reward: you have found precisely what you were looking for, even if the discovery itself tries to deny it.

Where to look: reach the top of the bridge tower on the San Fierro side. A jetpack makes the visit much easier.

Documentation: GTA-SanAndreas.com

06 / GTA IV

GTA IV: Liberty City has a heart, and it is chained up

The Statue of Happiness holds one of the series' strangest secrets: a gigantic beating heart suspended inside it by chains.

To reach it, use a helicopter to get onto the raised platform at the statue's feet. One of the doors can be passed through despite appearing closed. A ladder beyond it leads up into the interior.

The remarkable part is the change in tone. You come from a city of traffic, buildings and human conflicts, then suddenly find yourself inside a monument that seems to be alive.

The game leaves room for interpretation. You might read it as a grotesque image of the city's heart, or simply a macabre joke. It does not need a definitive explanation to make an impression.

Where to look: visit Happiness Island. The hidden entrance is on the raised platform at the statue's feet, beside a sign denying that there is hidden content.

Documentation: GameSpot · Grand Theft Wiki

07 / GTA V · Alien

GTA V: the alien you can miss before the game has really begun

GTA V earns two stops on this tour because it can hide one surprise during a getaway and reserve another for a specific hour of the night.

During the escape in North Yankton's prologue, a detour onto the frozen river reveals an alien trapped beneath the ice near the bridge.

It is an excellent place for a secret. While the story urges you to escape, the scenery holds an image that seems completely unrelated to the robbery. You can drive past the game's first major curiosity before you have even started looking for them.

Where to look: during the prologue getaway, detour onto the frozen river beneath the bridge. Be quick: staying off the intended route can fail the mission.

Documentation: Dynasty · 2013 · GamesRadar+

08 / GTA V · Ghost

GTA V: a late-night appointment with Mount Gordo's ghost

A ghostly figure appears on Mount Gordo between 23:00 and 00:00 on the in-game clock, above a rock bearing the name “Jock”.

Here, the reward depends on watching and waiting. The landscape stops being somewhere to travel through and becomes a place to revisit at a precise time.

The alien and the ghost illustrate two ways to surprise a player: hide something where everyone is rushing past, and put something else where almost nobody has a reason to stop.

Where to look: visit Mount Gordo between 23:00 and 00:00 in Story Mode. These are game hours, not real-world hours.

Documentation: GamesRadar+

Why we keep looking for GTA secrets

A good Easter egg changes how we see the rest of the map. Once you have walked through a false window, the other windows seem less final. After finding a heart inside a statue, any monument can look suspicious.

GTA's secrets work because they produce different reactions. Gouranga and the Elvis impersonators use dark humour. The signs in GTA III and San Andreas tease our persistence. Vice City plays with language. GTA IV and GTA V introduce images capable of unsettling us.

They do not all need an explanation or have to lead to something bigger. Sometimes a secret room contains nothing but a chocolate egg. And that is enough to make us check left the next time a mission tells us to turn right.
